Result
The opaque pistol muzzle-flash card is corrected in the tested scene. The
Lambda entrance corridor now has authored path-traced lighting. A visual survey
covers six camera locations on each of five maps, plus a pistol comparison.
This is not full-campaign lighting support or an end-to-end playthrough.

Changes
1. The animated muzzle-flash Spritecard materials now expose texture-times-
   vertex alpha to Remix and use SRCALPHA/ONE additive blending. Source normally
   performs ADDSELF premultiplication inside its pixel shader; forwarding its
   ONE/INVSRCALPHA state without that shader operation left an opaque card as
   the effect faded. The compatibility translation retains the vertex shader
   so atlas UVs and particle orientation continue to be captured correctly.
   The observed vistasmokev1 Spritecard also receives texture-times-vertex alpha.
   All modified render/texture-stage states use the existing per-pass restore.
   This is an approximation of the additive effect, not a full implementation
   of Source's dual-sequence, depth-fade, and frame-interpolation pixel shaders.
2. bm_c3a2a gets 16 shaped lights at eight entrance wall fixtures. Separate
   downward and upward lobes illuminate the walkway and curved ceiling. Other
   maps and existing light entries are unchanged. The fixture strips still need
   an emissive-material pass for closer visual fidelity.
3. Optional -rtx-material-diagnostics writes bounded source/prepared particle
   state information to rtx_comp/logs/particle-passes.log. It is disabled by
   default and does not itself change rendering.

Capture method and limits
The local evidence folder contains 93 original JPEGs: 30 static-view triplets
and one pistol triplet. Sorting by filename groups the three variants:
  <map>_<location>_01_rtx_before.jpg
  <map>_<location>_02_rtx_after.jpg
  <map>_<location>_03_vanilla.jpg

Before uses the published build-19042901 port, prior to this milestone's two
rendering changes. After uses the final milestone ASI and Lambda light entry.
Vanilla disables both local Remix/ASI loader DLLs and uses the game's native
renderer (including its bundled DXVK); no compatibility ASI was loaded.

The registry is tools/blackmesa/campaign-checkpoints.json. Each position is an
eye position returned by getpos. For a standing, noclip camera, subtract 64
from Z before using setpos_exact, then apply setang_exact. fov_desired was 90,
HUD/flashlight were off, and the viewmodel was off except in the pistol test.
All 30 static views matched recorded XYZ to the printed engine precision;
maximum angle difference was 0.005493 degrees due to angle quantization.
Pistol starting pose is matched, but recoil, particle phase and shot timing
are not frame-synchronized. Its three pictures are qualitative effect checks.

Checkpoints and the game's -hijack +exec interface were used to replay poses;
noclip, pause, and some god/notarget use isolated camera checks from movement
and combat. Later views within a map used teleports. NPCs, scripted state,
animation, particles, and exposure history can differ between variants.
This does not certify puzzles, transitions, enemy encounters, or completion.
The first Xen checkpoint came from the user-supplied save collection.

The game ran at 1280x720 windowed. Computer Use delivered 642x385 JPEG captures
including the title bar; files preserve those pixels, with no enhancement,
recoloring, cropping or resizing. These are not full-resolution pixel-diff
benchmarks. Some location labels are survey identifiers rather than room names.
Game saves, extracted game materials/maps, raw logs, and the POC asset pack are
not distributed in this repository. The camera registry alone cannot restore
an identical scripted scene; use corresponding local saves where available.

Findings by map
bm_c1a0b â€” Laboratory
  Existing POC lighting makes the human laboratory readable. RTX shifts color
  balance and misses some monitor/fixture glow. The embedded Xen vignette has
  severely overbright opaque particle cards; the narrow muzzle fix does not
  cover those materials. Priorities: classify the remaining effect materials
  and preserve their alpha/emission behavior, then tune material roughness and
  monitor/fixture emission against vanilla.

bm_c1a1a â€” Post-disaster laboratory
  The wrecked chamber loses much of vanilla's warm fire/emergency illumination.
  In contrast, several surrounding corridors are too bright and neutral in RTX
  compared with vanilla's dim red emergency lighting. Some white particle cards
  remain. Priorities: event-aware light states, fire/emergency emitters, and the
  relevant particle shaders. A global exposure increase would not solve this.

bm_c2a5a â€” Surface Tension
  Geometry and the playable foreground render, but RTX has a strong blue cast,
  weak warm sunlight, conspicuous sky boundaries, and missing distant scenery
  relative to vanilla. The entrance is also underlit. Priorities: diagnose the
  sky/3D-sky capture and culling paths, set a correct sun/environment, then check
  water, vegetation alpha and exterior material response. Missing distant
  scenery is an observed symptom, not a proven single culling defect.

bm_c3a2a â€” Lambda Complex
  The entrance was nearly black with clearly visible unlit fixtures. The new
  shaped lights make its walkway and wall arches legible. Four nearby/lower
  samples retain their existing lighting; deeper areas still need comparison
  and authoring. Priorities: emissive fixture surfaces, localized lighting in
  remaining rooms, and tests of doors, lifts and light-state transitions.

bm_c4a1a â€” Early Xen
  Five views are almost black in RTX. Vanilla shows a bright purple/cyan sky,
  colored environmental illumination, vegetation and readable paths. The sixth
  view's red hanging organism illuminates nearby rock, showing that some
  emissive effects work. Priorities: restore sky/environment capture, verify
  visibility/culling, translate alien emissive materials, and author biome
  lighting. These captures do not establish that all dark geometry is absent.
